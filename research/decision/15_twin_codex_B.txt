1. **AccessBond — accessibility promises with teeth.** Event organizers lock a deposit against specific promises such as a working lift or step-free entrance; a Soroban escrow releases refunds when a designated independent verifier confirms a failure. Demo one event, one promise, and one refund.

2. **Last Bolt — rescue repairs stuck on one missing part.** Repair cafés post bounties for exact spare parts, letting someone with a broken appliance supply the component that saves another. Soroban holds the bounty until the repairer confirms the part fits, while Stellar path payments let the supplier receive a different asset from the payer’s.

3. **ShiftBack — childcare insurance for canceled shifts.** Employers pre-fund a small guarantee that reimburses childcare costs when they cancel a worker’s shift at short notice. A Soroban contract handles the cancellation deadline and payout, with worker–employer multisig approval for disputed cases.

4. **ColdChain Commons — share a fridge before food spoils.** Market vendors reserve spare refrigerated space from nearby businesses during equipment failures. Stellar-issued assets represent dated storage slots, and Soroban escrow splits payment between reservation and a mutually confirmed handover.

5. **Quiet Hours — sponsor the business case for accessibility.** Families collectively fund a sensory-friendly session at a cinema, gym, or museum, but nobody pays unless the venue commits and the minimum funding threshold is met. Soroban provides conditional crowdfunding and refunds; issued assets serve as session passes.

6. **Return Relay — recover belongings without exposing either person’s address.** A finder drops a lost item at an agreed café or hostel, which confirms receipt and earns a handling fee alongside the finder’s reward. A Soroban escrow divides the bounty after the owner supplies a one-time collection code.

7. **Proof of Cleanup — make construction deposits useful.** Small renovation crews post a waste-disposal bond that pays out only after the customer and an approved disposal site attest to completion. A Stellar multisig account requires both attestations, turning an easily ignored disposal promise into a funded commitment.

8. **Seat Switch — swap appointments without selling them.** People exchange inconvenient appointments through two-way or three-way swaps, with the provider retaining control over eligibility. Provider-issued, authorization-required assets represent slots; one atomic Stellar transaction completes the entire swap or changes nothing.

9. **Exit Pool — make leaving shared housing less financially brutal.** Housemates gradually fund a pool that advances an outgoing tenant’s deposit while they wait for the landlord’s repayment. Soroban records contributions, caps advances, and requires housemate approval; demo the contribution → advance → repayment cycle.

10. **Deadline Rescue — buy the last missing contribution, not another subscription.** A student team, community newspaper, or volunteer group funds a tightly scoped rescue task—caption this video, translate this page, fix this diagram—with a named reviewer. After approval, a Stellar claimable balance lets the contributor collect before a deadline, with a separate refund predicate allowing the group to reclaim uncollected funds afterward.
