### Security observations that change the ranking

**Verified:** The [operation reference](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/transactions/list-of-operations) assigns Low to both ClaimClaimableBalance and BumpSequence, while Payment is Medium; it permits a bump up to INT64_MAX. **Verified:** The [fee-bump guide](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/fee-bump-transactions) requires only the fee account's low threshold. **Assumption — inference:** WI-2's guardian can therefore authorize fees against the parent's XLM and disrupt normal parent-sourced transactions. Denying a Payment proves only that one operation is denied. Do not install this guardian on a valuable account under a claim-only promise.

**Verified:** [Claimable-balance documentation](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/claimable-balances) makes the claim's operation source the receiving claimant and uses ledger time for predicates. **Assumption — inference:** A sender with an unconditional reclaim predicate can race the recipient after the delay; a delay is not a guarantee of eventual payment. A genuine cancellation-only delegation needs narrower authorization than an ordinary low-weight signer.

**Verified:** [Asset controls](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/control-asset-access) support the authorization sandwich in SA-2. **Assumption — inference:** Protocol enforcement of the issuer's signature is not protocol verification of a fair off-chain price. Issuer compromise, side payments and account sales remain outside that guarantee.

## 4. Build decision and scope

**Assumption — recommendation:** Build Heirloom for Unlocked. One owner, one recovery contact, one initialized classic account, one recovery artifact, one inactivity window. Start with XLM and a disposable testnet account; add a classic asset only after the main flow works. Require a simple, inspected signer/threshold configuration. Do not promise compatibility with arbitrary existing multisig or all Soroban token authorization models.

**Assumption — required corrections:** Call it inactivity recovery, not a legally effective will. Only relevant source-account sequence activity refreshes the timer; receiving a payment does not establish owner liveness, and an operation submitted under another transaction source is not necessarily a heartbeat. The contact gains powerful account access after recovery; disclose that an alive but inactive owner can lose control. Initialization must create a fresh sequence timestamp. Reserve, signer-slot, changed-threshold and stale-XDR failures must be visible; an included failed transaction may consume its sequence. Adding the heir requires reserve headroom, and fee sponsorship does not supply that reserve.

**Assumption — next-hour gate:** Prove early rejection, an owner-sourced heartbeat resetting the delay, later recovery through a fee bump, and explicit revocation rejecting the old artifact. Inspect the actual signer state after recovery. Test the real wallet's ability to sign the unusual sequence/preconditions. If that cannot be demonstrated within one focused hour, switch to Loop and prove a three-party cycle before building its graph. Do not spend the remaining day repairing obscure wallet integration.

**Assumption — backup scope:** Loop should show three firms, one clearly consented cycle, a before/after ledger graph and one explorer transaction. Remove residual fiat settlement, invoice uploads, privacy and claims that the ledger alone settles legal debts. Do not confuse gross obligations cancelled with cash saved: three equal debts of 100 cleared together are 300 of gross obligations, not 300 of required initial liquidity.

**Speculation:** Heirloom's continuation path is a wallet recovery feature; Loop's is an opt-in business netting group. Neither has validated distribution or willingness to pay.

## 5. Three critical Heirloom assumptions

1. **Verified — protocol semantics:** [CAP-21](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0021.md) specifies that `minSeqNum <= account.seqNum < transaction.seqNum` permits a future-sequence transaction and `minSeqAge` checks the source's sequence timestamp. A sufficiently distant sequence therefore survives ordinary increments until that upper bound. **Could-not-verify:** I have not executed this exact recovery construction on testnet in Stage 1.
2. **Verified — activity and cancellation primitives:** [CAP-21](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0021.md) and the [transaction guide](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/transactions/operations-and-transactions) define the sequence-based preconditions; the [BumpSequence reference](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/transactions/list-of-operations#bump-sequence) supplies the explicit revocation operation. **Assumption — inference:** Bumping to at least the saved transaction's sequence violates its strict upper bound. Initialize the account and test refresh behavior rather than relying on the phrase “any activity.” **Could-not-verify:** Wallet/UI behavior and boundary races need the live gate.
3. **Verified — controlled recovery and fee sponsorship:** [Multisig documentation](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/transactions/signatures-multisig) describes signer weights and thresholds, and the [fee-bump guide](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/fee-bump-transactions) permits a different account to pay for an already signed inner transaction. **Assumption — inference:** An owner-authorized SetOptions transaction can add the contact once valid, provided current authorization and reserves allow it; the fee wrapper does not relax the inner conditions. **Could-not-verify:** Freighter support for the constructed XDR, reserve failure handling, and arbitrary SEP-41 token behavior have not been tested and are not promised.

**Assumption — final blind judgment:** Heirloom's advantage is a clear protocol-enforced condition with a small build surface. Loop is the more visually surprising backup. The original Second Thought guardian design should not be shipped without a narrower authorization model, regardless of its strong consumer story.
